openGym iOS redesign · Round 1

Pick the design system

The same Today screen with the same data, built in three systems. Tap around in each one: pick a day, start the workout, log a weigh-in. Lime is openGym's existing brand color, so it stays the accent in all three to keep the comparison fair.

Option 1

Apple HIG, refined My pick

Native iOS structure (large titles, grouped cards, SF Pro with rounded numbers) plus lime for progress and actions only.

Best for: a long-lived, dense app. It scales to Plan, Stats and Settings without fighting the OS. Risk: it can look generic unless the numbers and the accent carry the character.

Option 2

Athletic bold

Condensed uppercase type, giant numbers, a solid lime hero block, sharp corners. High energy, made for the gym floor.

Best for: the live workout screen and big at-a-glance numbers. Risk: gets loud and tiring on data-heavy screens like Plan, History and Settings.

Option 3

Tool-dark (Linear-style)

Near-black surfaces with hairline borders, Geist plus monospace numbers, small uppercase labels. Calm, precise, dense.

Best for: power users who track lots of data. Risk: it feels like a dev tool, cool and not very motivating for a fitness app.

Round 1b · Hybrids

Options 1 and 3, with option 2's titles and numbers

Same screens, same colors and shapes as 1 and 3. Only the page title, workout name and numbers switch to the bold condensed style. Body text, labels and buttons stay as they were.

Option 1b

Apple HIG + bold numbers

Native iOS cards and spacing, with condensed uppercase titles and large numbers for weight, streak and the workout name.

Gives the native layout a sporty voice without option 2's loud lime block. Risk: the condensed font is not a system font, so it has to ship with the app.

Option 3b

Tool-dark + bold numbers

Hairline borders and a dense layout, with condensed uppercase titles and large numbers in place of the monospace numbers.

Warmer and more motivating than plain 3. Risk: two strong type voices (small uppercase labels and big condensed numbers) compete on busy screens.
