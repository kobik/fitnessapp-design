openGym redesign · Plan tab · iPhone

Plan: the week and the routines

The Plan tab answers two questions: what do I train on which day, and what is in each routine. Below are three directions, each a working phone. Use the switch to put all three in the same situation. Inside a phone you can tap days, open routines, change sets, reps, weight and rest, add or remove exercises, delete a routine and load a starter plan. Every delete and every discard asks first. The sample week is the same one Today uses: Legs on Monday (done), Push A today (Wednesday), Pull A on Friday, Legs on Saturday.

Same in all three

  • Color only marks a routine. Push lime, Pull cyan, Legs orange. A routine you make yourself is grey (picking a color for it is new, see below). Today, changes, "Up next" and the solid button are white.
  • Red only on destructive actions: Delete routine, the list's delete minus and the Delete button in the question. Taking a routine out of the loop uses a grey minus, because it can be undone. This is the iOS standard, not decoration.
  • Unsaved is always visible. A changed value gets a white rule, ring or dot, plus how many changes are waiting, until you save or discard.
  • Ask before losing anything. Deleting a routine, discarding changes, Undo all and starting the loop over all ask first. Taking a routine out of the loop, or off a day in C, shows an Undo instead: nothing is deleted, and the routine stays.
  • One solid button per screen (Load starter plan, Save, Done in a sheet). Everything else is outlined or text.
  • Exercise stills. Every exercise row in the editor has a small still, and the open exercise shows a bigger one; tap it for a sheet with the moving animation, the name and a three-step how-to. The figure is a stand-in for the app's own clips.
  • Same numbers as Today. Push A has 6 exercises and 20 sets, Pull A 6 and 20, Legs 5 and 19. The weights and rests are sample targets, the same on every phone.
  • The tab bar is the locked capsule with Plan selected. The GO disc takes the color of today's routine (grey when nothing is planned today). B's editor covers it, like an iOS full-screen edit.
  • A copied routine (Routine options › Copy routine in the editor) is grey until it gets its own color, so two routines never share one.

What the real app supports

  • Two ways to train: Fixed week, or Rotation (a loop of routines and rest days, with Done / Up next / Later, "1 of 3 done this round" and Start the loop over). Fixed-day routines can ride along beside a loop.
  • A day can hold several routines, trained together in the order picked. Tap a day, pick one or more, or Rest day.
  • Routines: a name and icon, a progression rule, a deload switch, exercises with sets × reps, weight, rest, warm-ups, per side, last set to failure, pyramids, drop sets, backups and notes. Also supersets (A1, A2), Replace exercise, Copy, Print / Save as PDF and Delete.
  • Plan menu: Export plan file, Print, Import a plan file, Load starter plan. The four starter plans can be fitted to an equipment profile (not shown here).
  • Reordering by long-press drag, plus up and down arrows. The mock uses arrows only.

New for the app

  • Asking before a delete from the list. Today the list's minus and swipe delete at once with an Undo toast; only the routine page asks. Here every delete asks.
  • Draft editing with a Save step (all three). The app saves every change in the routine editor at once today.
  • "Discard N changes?" when you leave the editor with unsaved changes, plus the save bar and the white rules on changed sets. Today the app's exercise sheet closes without a word and the edits are lost.
  • A color per routine. The app gives each routine an icon. The redesign's color system needs a color per routine, and a way to pick one for routines you make.
  • Day buttons on each routine (C) and Undo after a day change. The app assigns days through the day sheet only.
  • Asking before Start the loop over. The app restarts the round with no question.
  • "Wednesday becomes a rest day" in the delete question. The app's message names only the routine.
